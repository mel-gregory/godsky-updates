Orbital Network

Feed Health — is Earth's data flowing?

Orbital Network does not measure the planet. It measures the planet's measuring systems. This page is the live state of that instrument: how fast the world's official agencies publish, how fresh their data is when it reaches us, what our provenance rules refuse, and which stations have gone silent. Orbital Network operates no satellites or ground stations of its own. It republishes, links, and permanently records the official reports of the systems that do. Orbital Network is an independent project — not a government agency. It issues no forecasts or warnings — consult NWS/NHC/local authorities for warnings and safety decisions.

      How to read this page

      Latency
      Republication latency: how long after the source's own timestamp a record entered our store. The median is the typical record; p90 is the slower end — nine records in ten arrived faster.
      Stale at ingest
      The share of station readings whose observation was already more than 6 hours old when it reached us — the same threshold the public site uses to label data outdated. Counted, not hidden.
      Quarantine
      Records our rules refuse: a timestamp that could not be true, a missing identifier, coordinates that cannot exist. They are kept aside for diagnosis and counted above.
      Silence
      A station that stops reporting leaves no announcement — it simply stops. We track the gap from the station's last received report.

      Nothing on this page is estimated, sampled, or carried in from outside: every number is an aggregate over Orbital Network's own event store, republished from official sources — USGS, NOAA (NHC and NDBC), NASA EONET, GDACS, and USGS NWIS. Orbital Network reports on the instruments; official hazard warnings remain the agencies' own.
